# Story Structures (Internal Variant)

Internal communication does not need narrative arcs the way investor pitches do. The structures here are functional — they organize information for a specific decision-making purpose. Pick the structure that matches the level and meeting type.

## Internal Sync (team / department, weekly–monthly)

For team weekly, biweekly, and department monthly check-ins. Slide flow:

1. Cover (1)
2. Last period progress (1–2): what shipped, what hit milestones
3. Current period goals (1–2): what we are working toward this period
4. Blockers and dependencies (1)
5. KPI snapshot (1): just the headline numbers
6. Numbers tied to systems — every figure with a source
7. Action items / asks (1)

Time: 15–30 min team weekly; 30–60 min department monthly.
Slide count: 5–10 (team), 10–15 (department).
Tone: working, can use jargon, can have detail.

## Board Update (board quarterly)

For quarterly board meetings. This is the most-used structure for board-level work. Slide flow:

1. Cover with period and meeting date (1)
2. Agenda (1)
3. KPI vs Plan (1–2): big visible table with status colors
4. Top 3 wins (1)
5. Top 3 misses with root cause (1–2): direct, no whitewashing
6. **Surprises this quarter (mandatory section)** (1): events not anticipated last quarter — competitive, regulatory, customer, internal
7. **Competitor analysis and 36-month moat re-check** (2–3): if any surprise touches competition or technology landscape, the moat re-check is mandatory; the weighted score with derivation appears in the deck
8. Strategy adjustments (1–2): how the response to misses and surprises shapes the next quarter
9. Capital and runway (1): cash position, burn vs plan, next-round timing
10. Risk register update (1): technical / talent / budget risks
11. **Decision items for the board (mandatory)** (1–2): explicit list, each item with proposed action and what is being asked of the board
12. Q+1 commitments (1): what the team commits to deliver by next board meeting
13. Open Q&A (1)

Time: 60–120 min, depending on whether annual planning is included.
Slide count: 15–25 for quarterly; 25–30 for annual.
Tone: strategic, avoid operational detail unless needed for context.

**Required sections** in this structure:
- KPI vs Plan
- Top 3 wins / Top 3 misses with root cause
- Surprises (every surprise tagged with "moat re-check triggered? Y / N + reason")
- Decision items

Without these, it is not a board update — it is a status report dressed in board clothes.

## All-hands narrative (all-hands quarterly / half-yearly)

For company-wide all-hands meetings. Slide flow:

1. Cover (1)
2. Vision recap (1): one slide reaffirming the why
3. Quarter wins (2–3): projects shipped, milestones hit, customer stories
4. Numbers overview (1): high-level company KPIs, with context
5. The one big thing this quarter (1): the most important development to celebrate or rally around
6. Challenges we are facing (1–2): the misses or hard situations, with the response — never present misses without saying what is being done
7. New people / promotions (1): humanize, build community
8. Roadmap preview (1–2): what is coming in the next quarter
9. Recognition / culture moment (1): a story or callout that reinforces values
10. Q&A (1)

Time: 30–60 min.
Slide count: 10–15.
Tone: narrative, motivational, but not vacuous. The team can detect manufactured optimism.

**Required sections** in this structure:
- A response framing for any miss — never just "things are hard" without "and here is what we are doing"
- A connection back to the company's why — the all-hands is the moment to renew shared purpose
- Q&A time — at least 10 minutes, ideally 15

## Strategy off-site (annual / semi-annual leadership)

For annual planning or strategy reviews with the leadership team. Mixed structure — borrows from Why Now (when discussing market context), Board Update (when reviewing the past period), and Action Plan formats. Slide flow varies.

If running a strategy off-site, ask the user explicitly which sub-structures they want and assemble accordingly.

Slide count: 25–40 across the off-site (often spread over 1–2 days).

## A note on duration

Internal meetings tend to expand to fill the time. The slide count in each structure is the *target* — going materially over indicates either too much content or too little editing. Push back on the user if they ask for "more slides" without a specific reason.
