# Anti-Pattern Library — Internal Variant

Internal communication has its own failure modes, distinct from investor pitches. Walk this checklist before delivering any internal deck.

## Internal-specific anti-patterns (highest priority)

### 1. Whitewashing misses to spare board / team feelings

**Trigger**: A miss is described in language that obscures its scale. "We are slightly behind on partnerships" when the actual number is -40%. "Burn was a little over plan" when the actual number is +30%.
**Why it fails**: Board members and team members can read numbers. Soft language signals that the founder is either out of touch or hiding things — both are corrosive to trust. The next miss gets harder to disclose because the previous one was muddied.
**Fix**: State the miss directly with the number. Then state the root cause. Then state the response. "Partnerships missed -40%, root cause was procurement-cycle underestimation, response is to budget 6–8 weeks for legal review going forward and adjust Q2 target."

### 2. Surprise without action plan

**Trigger**: Bad news is delivered without a paired statement of what is being done about it. The deck says "Schrödinger shipped a competing feature" or "we lost our largest customer" and then moves on.
**Why it fails**: This is the worst failure mode for board updates and all-hands. The audience walks away with anxiety and no anchor. Board members cannot help; team members cannot focus.
**Fix**: Every piece of bad news must be paired with response framing. Even if the response is "we are still figuring out the right answer," that is better than silence. "Schrödinger shipped a competing feature with 40% overlap. We are running the moat re-check this week (see Slide X) and will return to the board with a strategy adjustment proposal by end of next quarter."

### 3. Surprises not tagged with moat re-check status

**Trigger**: A surprise involving competitive landscape is reported but no moat re-check is referenced.
**Why it fails**: The surprise gets observed and forgotten. Three quarters later, the company is materially behind and no one can pinpoint when the gap opened.
**Fix**: Every surprise gets tagged: "Moat re-check triggered: Y / N + reason." If Y, the re-check is a chapter in the same deck. If N, the reason is briefly stated. This is mandatory at board level.

### 4. Vague decision items

**Trigger**: Board update lists "discuss strategy for X" or "explore Y" as decision items.
**Why it fails**: These are not decision items. The board cannot decide on a discussion. The meeting time gets eaten on rehashing what should have been pre-read.
**Fix**: Decision items should be concrete and actionable: "Approve $120K capex for Y," "Approve hire of senior medchem advisor at $8K/month," "Approve early Seed round launch in Q2 mid." Each item with proposed action and what is being asked.

### 5. All-hands without an emotional through-line

**Trigger**: All-hands deck reads like a department review — facts, KPIs, plans — with no narrative thread tying the company's purpose to the quarter's events.
**Why it fails**: All-hands is the moment to renew shared purpose. A facts-only deck wastes that moment and leaves the team less connected, not more.
**Fix**: Use the All-hands narrative structure. Every all-hands should have a "vision recap" slide, a "the one big thing" slide, and a "recognition / culture moment" slide. These are not fluff — they are the entire point of the meeting format.

### 6. Manufactured optimism in all-hands

**Trigger**: All-hands deck describes a hard quarter as "exciting" and full of "growth opportunities" without acknowledging the reality.
**Why it fails**: Teams detect this immediately. Cynicism is the response. Subsequent all-hands meetings have lower attention even when the news is real.
**Fix**: Be honest about hard things. Pair them with response. Trust the team to handle reality.

### 7. Pre-loading the board with detail and leaving no time for discussion

**Trigger**: 25-slide board update with dense content, leaving 10 minutes for Q&A in a 90-minute meeting.
**Why it fails**: The board's value is judgment and discussion, not having facts read to them. A board meeting that is mostly presentation underuses the room.
**Fix**: Pre-read materials handle the dense content. The meeting itself focuses on decision items, surprises, and strategy adjustments. Aim for 30–40% of meeting time on discussion.

### 8. KPI reported without prior baseline

**Trigger**: A board update presents KPIs without comparing them to the prior quarter's plan or the operating plan.
**Why it fails**: Without baseline, the audience cannot judge whether the KPI is good or bad. "ARR is $250K" — is that on plan? Behind? Ahead? The board cannot tell.
**Fix**: Always show vs Plan. If this is the first board meeting and no prior plan exists, mark Plan as 🔴 needs-data and propose that future meetings have explicit Q-on-Q comparison.

## Inherited general anti-patterns

The general anti-patterns from external pitches still apply at the strategic-discussion level (annual planning, Why-Now framing for strategy off-sites). When discussing market context in any internal deck:

- **Avoid over-large TAM claims** in strategy discussions — internal audiences will be more skeptical, not less, of an inflated TAM
- **Use bottom-up SOM logic** for any market-sizing discussion
- **Apply the 36-month moat check honestly** — internal audiences should see the unflattering version

## SaaS-specific anti-patterns

- **Hiding customer concentration from the board** — if any single customer is >30% of ARR, the board needs to know
- **Reporting ARR without churn** — net retention without gross churn is misleading
- **Pretty cohort retention chart that excludes early cohorts** — full data or none

## Biotech-specific anti-patterns

- **Underplaying regulatory delays** to the board — board members can absorb bad news; they cannot absorb being surprised by news the founder already knew
- **Treating FTO concerns as something to deal with later** — surface early, get help from board's legal connections
- **Excluding reimbursement from board updates** — for any therapeutic / diagnostic / SaMD with insurance-paid customers, reimbursement is a board-level concern

## How to walk the checklist

Before delivering any internal deck, walk these patterns. For each one, either confirm "did not happen" or note where the deck addresses it. The Anti-pattern Self-check Results section at the bottom of the deck should explicitly list each pattern and the verdict.
