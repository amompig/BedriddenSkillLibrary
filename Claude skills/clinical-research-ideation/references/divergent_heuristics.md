# Divergent ideation heuristics (Round 2 of staged mode)

When generating 8–12 candidate topics in staged Round 2, apply these heuristics to ensure diversity and avoid clustering.

## Coverage rules

Cover at least:
- **3 of {operator-dependency, workflow throughput, downstream decision}** pain-point types
- **2 different POCUS modalities** (FAST, lung, cardiac, RUSH, eFAST, etc.)
- **1 "low-tech high-feasibility" candidate** — pure claims, no new imaging cohort
- **1 "high-tech high-risk" candidate** — foundation model, federated, novel architecture
- **1 "policy-leverage" candidate** — designed for MOHW rather than NSTC; outcomes / health-services framing

## Avoid clustering

- Avoid having all candidates around the same modality
- Avoid having all candidates use the same ML method
- Avoid having all candidates target the same funding mechanism
- Avoid having all candidates with the same SPOF pattern

## Why divergence matters

Reviewers (and funders) compare candidates. If 8 candidates all look similar, the user has effectively generated 1 idea with 8 surface variations. The point of divergent ideation is to surface the surrounding decision space so that the down-selection in Round 3 is meaningful.

## Negative constraints (extended)

- DO NOT recommend saturated topics (BUSI re-classification, EchoNet re-implementation) without a clear differentiator
- DO NOT conflate technical AUROC with clinical utility — always separate the two
- DO NOT claim global novelty if the topic is only TW-novel, or vice versa — be precise about regional scope
- DO NOT propose data the user cannot realistically obtain
- DO NOT propose timelines that ignore the HWDC + IRB lead time
- DO NOT score a 5 on any criterion without explicit justification — 5 means best-in-class, not "good"
- DO NOT cluster all 8–12 candidates around one modality or method
