# Story Structures (External / Investor)

Pick one primary structure based on situation. The structure determines slide order and emphasis — but the mandatory chapters (competitor analysis, biotech subflow if applicable) are inserted regardless.

## Selection guidance

Ask the user about their situation, then suggest one structure (occasionally two for them to compare). Do not just default to Problem-Solution-Market — it is the most common and the most overused.

| Structure | Best when | Worst when |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Problem-Solution-Market | Audience unfamiliar with the domain; classic Seed / Series A | Audience already knows the problem deeply |
| Why Now | Industry is at a transition point and timing is the hook | Story does not actually need a "now" — feels forced |
| Category Creation | Founder has strong narrative skills and the company is genuinely creating a new category | Trying to inflate a niche product into a "category" — investors see through this |
| Founder Story | Deep tech, biotech-from-academia, or any case where the founder *is* the unique edge | Generic founder background — no individual edge to lead with |

## 1. Problem-Solution-Market

The default for unfamiliar-domain audiences. Slide flow:

1. Title — one-line positioning
2. Problem — concrete, painful, who has it, how often
3. Solution — what you built, demo if possible
4. Market — TAM/SAM/SOM with bottom-up SOM logic
5. Traction — what proves the solution works
6. **Competitor analysis (mandatory)** — see `competitor_moat_check.md`
7. Team
8. **Biotech subflow if applicable** — see `biotech_subflow.md`
9. Ask — amount, use of funds, milestones, timeline
10. Vision

## 2. Why Now

Use when the legitimate hook is timing — a regulatory shift, a tech maturation, a market transition. Slide flow:

1. Title
2. **Why Now** — the industry transition, with verified data
3. Where the opportunity is — what new behavior or product becomes possible
4. Our angle — how we are positioned for this moment
5. Solution + early evidence
6. **Competitor analysis (mandatory)** — frame as "who else sees this and what they have / lack"
7. Team — emphasize timing-related expertise
8. **Biotech subflow if applicable**
9. Traction
10. Ask

The Why Now data on Slide 2 must be verified — this is the structural keystone, and weak data here collapses the whole pitch.

## 3. Category Creation

Use when the founder is genuinely creating a new category and has the narrative skill to land it. Slide flow:

1. Title
2. The old paradigm — and why it is failing
3. The new category — name it, define it
4. We are the creator of this category
5. Evidence — early customers, design partners, quantified shifts
6. **Competitor analysis (mandatory)** — frame as "why this category was not built earlier and what we have that they do not"
7. **Biotech subflow if applicable**
8. Team — emphasize category-defining expertise
9. Vision and Ask

Risk: investors are very tired of "category creation" claims. Walk away from this structure unless the user has unusual narrative ability AND the category is genuinely new.

## 4. Founder Story

Use when the founder *is* the unique edge — deep tech, biotech spun out of academia, science-driven companies, or any case where one or two people's expertise is the moat. Slide flow:

1. Title
2. Personal background — relevant credentials and experience
3. The problem I saw — concrete moment or experience
4. Why only I can solve this — domain access, IP, network, specific expertise
5. The company — what we built and what we are doing
6. **Competitor analysis (mandatory)** — frame as "why others have not / could not"
7. Solution and traction
8. Team
9. **Biotech subflow if applicable** (very common with this structure)
10. Ask

This structure should not be used to glorify the founder. The pitch is still about the company; the founder story is the entry door, not the entire deck.

## Slide count by duration

| Duration | Slide count target | Notes |
|----------|-------------------|-------|
| 5 min (Angel quick pitch) | 5–7 | Cut to title, problem, solution, traction, ask. Trust the rest to follow-up meeting. |
| 10–12 min (Angel formal) | 10–12 | Full structure, light on detail. |
| 15 min (VC standard) | 12–15 | Full structure with breathing room for Q&A. |
| 20+ min (board / strategic) | 15–20 | Full structure with appendix slides for diligence-level data. |

A reasonable rule: ~1 slide per minute, with a 1–2 slide buffer for transitions.
