# Part A → Part D coupling rule

**Every reference appearing in Part A must be cited in at least one Part D candidate's novelty / prior-work section.**

If a reference does not justify any candidate, remove it from Part A.

## Why this rule exists

Without coupling, Part A devolves into an encyclopedic literature dump disconnected from the ideation output. The Part A → Part D linkage forces every cited paper to earn its place by either:
- (a) demonstrating prior work that a candidate differentiates from, OR
- (b) providing a methodological template that a candidate adopts.

## How to apply in practice

1. After drafting Part A and Part D, walk Part A row by row.
2. For each reference, identify which Part D candidate's "Novelty vs prior work" section cites it.
3. If none, either:
   - add it to a relevant candidate's novelty discussion, OR
   - remove the reference from Part A.
4. After this walk, every Part A row should be traceable to ≥1 Part D candidate.

## Negative constraints (extended)

- Do not pad Part A with references unrelated to candidates
- Do not cite without a verification tag
- Do not use bare URLs as citations — extract DOI / PMID / arXiv ID
- Do not conflate preprint with peer-reviewed status — flag preprints explicitly in the Venue column
- Do not cite Wikipedia, news articles, or marketing pages as primary references for clinical claims
- Do not cite a single review article as standalone evidence for a contested clinical claim — pair with primary studies
